The lower speedup on the cosine notebook comes from a different workload and substantial specialization gaps. The strongest evidence is that the cosine case activates zero uniform-material and paired-source fast classes, while the earlier Mie cube activates them extensively. Short profiling also shows heavy generic kernels and many small wavefront launches. This is not established as a pure DRAM-bandwidth or simple cell-count problem.

The historical 512³ Mie result was 98.742s versus 276.397s for pinned main (2.80×). That main revision was `d5282752`; it is not a new measurement of current main after preparation PR533. The current cosine result is 50.961s versus 71.835s for current main `387cc8fa` (1.41×). The two ratios are not a controlled regression comparison. On the same cosine case, the previous and reduced-memory candidates were50.554s and50.961s, a difference under1% across recorded runs; the memory changes do not explain the difference from the historical2–3× result.

Normalization by physical Yee updates also shows a real workload sensitivity: the Mie cube achieved about16.78GCUPS temporal throughput, while the cosine case achieves about7.14GCUPS. Main’s corresponding figures are about6.00 and5.07GCUPS, though its revisions differ. The cube has134.2M cells and12,347 steps; cosine has20.8M cells and17,484 steps. These are useful normalization figures, not an assertion of identical work per cell.

**Material representation is the clearest compiler gap.** The cosine uses lossless rank-two block headers and a shared literal/constant pool. It has1416,1431 and908 distinct FP32 E coefficients across its three components, so a global256-value byte dictionary cannot represent it exactly. Yet98.9–99.1% of each component’s physical coefficients equal the background value. The current native material certificate accepts only rank-one byte codes and tables of at most256 values. The NoIO and observer fast dispatches also require Layout 1. The block-packed scene therefore reports zero certified bulk, boundary and observer tiles. The cube reports24,745 bulk,17,452 NoIO and3,091 observer tiles in its corresponding uniform classes, plus paired-source specializations.

An independent exact-value decoder and conservative trajectory-box audit of the actual cosine inputs reproduces all six native policy counts. It proves eligibility, subject to block-format support, for 1,462 bulk tiles,5,888 NoIO boundary tiles and813 observer tiles—8,163 of 9,248 tiles. The audit keeps every interface coefficient, checks the full depth 16 trajectory support and filters empty E-update trajectories. These are potential dispatch facts, not a GPU implementation or a speedup. The most direct next change is to extend material fact extraction and the relevant dispatch guards to exact block-packed values. Preserve the generic kernel for mixed interfaces and verify all-array parity. This can recover scalar coefficient folding, compact register storage and cached CPML profiles without changing the physical mesh or quantizing materials.

**Shape amplifies boundary and scheduling costs.** With12 CPML cells on every face, the geometric absorber shell occupies33.8% of the481×481×90 grid versus13.4% of512³. This is a geometric proxy, not a measured fraction of runtime. Swept depth 16 dependency windows expand the affected tile cohorts further. Only 1,590 of 9,248 cosine tiles are pure bulk, versus 25,987 of 49,248 cube tiles. The source topology differs as well: cosine uses four native components (presence mask 54) and three frequency terms, while the existing paired-plane source fast class is guarded for mask 20, two contributors, and cube-like plane coverage. Its kernels cannot simply be reused by dropping the longitudinal mode components.

**There is evidence of limited residency and small launch cohorts.** A CUPTI activity trace of the unchanged final candidate over 1,024 steps has no dropped records. Dominant generic kernels launch 512 threads with 125–128 registers/thread and 51,748 bytes of dynamic shared memory. Queried RTX3090 limits are 82 SMs, 65,536 registers/SM, 102,400 shared bytes/SM and 1,536 threads/SM. For the128-register kernels, one 512-thread CTA already consumes the register file; two CTAs also exceed the shared-memory budget. Their theoretical warp-occupancy ceiling is therefore about 33%, before wavefront underfill. This is not an achieved-occupancy measurement. The existing uniform 1024-thread classes use 64 registers/thread and offer a different residency/work distribution.

About79.8% of the8,386 moving-kernel launches in the trace have fewer than 82 CTAs; their summed durations account for66.2% of summed moving-kernel duration. Up to six policy streams overlap, so those figures cannot be read as an idle-SM fraction. They show why total cell count alone does not guarantee saturation of the dependency-ordered schedule. The activity trace sums to7.224s of overlapping kernel durations over a2.947s kernel-interval union.

Summed kernel-duration shares in this short trace are approximately35% NoIO CPML/boundary,26% full general fallback,11% interior observation,18% interior source/source+observation,7% bulk and2% DFT accumulation. These are instrumented overlapping-kernel shares, not additive wall-time critical-path fractions.

Simply pruning nominally inactive or zero-weight plan entries barely reduces unique raw observations because adjacent samples share their dependencies. Disabling all monitors is only an upper-bound diagnostic; the notebook needs those outputs. It does not support attributing a potential2× gain to DFT accumulation. Shallower temporal blocks are not automatically better for this thin scene. Full-duration confirmation is required for any eventual optimization.

**Recommended order:**

1. Extend block-aware material facts and uniform kernel dispatch. This is a directly observed representation/dispatch gap with a large proved candidate set. Retain exact literal coefficients at interfaces; add negative controls for a one-bit coefficient change inside a trajectory.
2. Reprofile, then target generic source mask 54 and the measured small cohorts. Trace the real component mask, contributor count, phase timing and supports; select or generate matching kernels and selectively batch/fuse small ready cohorts without breaking dependencies.
3. Tune tile aspect ratios, storage axes and phase ownership for thin slabs. The current plane cache was developed around large cube workloads. Benchmark cyclic layouts and shape-specific index lowering, then evaluate whether changing spatial ownership can reduce boundary treatment and fill/drain costs. Depth4 already loses in the present screen.
4. Consider compact/ragged monitor metadata and more direct plane observation lowering primarily to recover memory headroom. Global row padding or a new layout can consume the current 46 MB GPU margin, so each proposal must retain the complete-process memory gate. Current main’s DFT path already uses packed launches for this mixed-frequency case.

A global XYZ transpose alone is not an established answer. The current X dimension is contiguous; another orientation trades coalescing, tile counts, source/observer topology and wavefront rank lengths. The more immediate layout issue is that a lossless coefficient format suppresses otherwise provable compiler facts. Register/shared allocation remains relevant, but accurate material/source facts should come before imposing register caps that may spill the cached fields.

Reaching 2× versus current main would require35.92s rather than50.96s, about29.5% less solver time. Reaching 3× requires23.95s, about53.0% less. The specialization gap makes further gains credible; neither target has been demonstrated on this notebook.
